# Fatigue Assessment of Railway Bridges to AS 5100

A technical research paper examining fatigue assessment procedures for steel railway bridges using the Australian Standard AS 5100.2-2017.

## Overview

This paper presents a detailed fatigue analysis of a 14.4-metre span steel plate through-girder rail underbridge carrying passenger-only traffic. It identifies several issues with the AS 5100 Bridge Design suite (2017) and proposes recommendations for more accurate fatigue life estimation.

## Key Findings

- **Stress cycle underestimation**: AS 5100.2-2017 can underestimate actual stress cycles when using the damage equivalence factor method alone.
- **Conservative remaining life**: The equivalent stress method can be overly conservative compared to damage summation (Palmgren-Miner rule).
- **S-N curve inconsistencies**: Mathematical errors exist in the S-N curve equations at the knee point for cycles exceeding 10^7.
- **Capacity reduction factor**: The standard applies the factor to S-N curves, whereas applying it to damage limits is more consistent with fatigue principles.

Despite theoretical 200LA design loading indicating end-of-life concerns, actual Waratah Electric loading demonstrates the bridge has 77-175+ years of remaining life after 25 years of service.
